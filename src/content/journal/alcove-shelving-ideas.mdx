Think about how you want to use your alcoves. Open shelves can display books and decorative pieces, while cupboards and drawers provide storage for items you prefer to keep out of sight. The things you plan to store will help determine shelf heights, depths and cupboard layouts.

Measure larger books, storage boxes and any equipment before finalising the design. If you have alcoves on both sides of a fireplace, the units can share the same overall style while having different internal layouts to suit your needs.

### How far apart should the shelves be?

Equal gaps look calm and orderly, and suit things of a similar size, like a run of books. Mixed gaps let you give one opening to a vase or a picture and keep the rest for books.

Leave room above your tallest item so you can lift it out. Adjustable shelves let you change the layout later; fixed shelves suit a display where the lighting is already planned. Many units use both: fixed shelves on show, adjustable ones inside the cupboards.

### Will the shelves bend?

A row of books weighs far more than a few vases, so a shelf is sized for what it will carry. Its material, its thickness, how far apart its supports are and how it is fixed all decide whether it stays straight.

A thicker-looking shelf is not always stronger. Some are made to look thick with a strip along the front, and what matters is what is inside. Wide shelves may need that front strip to stiffen them, or an extra support.

<Theme id="light" count="Lighting" title="Should the shelves be lit?" />

Lighting shows off what is on the shelves and gives a softer light in the evening. LED strips (thin strips of light set into the shelf) light along a shelf. Small downlights (spotlights set into the shelf above) pick out one section at a time.

You can light every bay, a few shelves, or just the main display. The photo at the top of this page has a downlight in every bay; idea 4 lights only the outer bays, so they draw the eye first.

For a living room, warm white works well: 2,700 to 3,000 K. The K measures how warm or cool a light looks, and lower is warmer, like an ordinary household bulb. Compare it with the other lights in the room.

<BearInMind>Lighting is planned with the units, not added after. The wires, switches and the small power unit the LEDs need all take space, and the power unit must stay reachable. Your quote should say which lighting and electrical work is included.</BearInMind>

**Uneven walls.** Older walls and chimney breasts are rarely straight, so measure both alcoves at several heights. The edges of each unit are trimmed to follow the wall, while the fronts stay in a straight line.

**Fireplaces and stoves.** If the fireplace is used, or there is a stove, its installer's safe distances must be checked before the sizes are agreed. An alcove being there does not mean it is far enough from the heat.

**Skirting, curtains and doors.** Allow for the skirting board, curtains, nearby doors and anything that needs to be reached for maintenance.
