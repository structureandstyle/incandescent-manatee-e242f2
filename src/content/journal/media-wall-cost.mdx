A media wall brings the television, its equipment and storage into one piece of fitted furniture. The room is no longer arranged around a black box on a stand. The cost depends on how much of the wall it covers and how much is built into it.

<Theme id="price" count="Starting price" title="What a media wall costs with us" />

Our bespoke media walls and built-in TV units start from £3,500. That guide price is for a complete unit, designed for the wall, delivered and fitted.

The final figure depends on the size, the design, the finish and the specification. A fixed, itemised quotation follows the measured survey, so you see what each part costs before anything is agreed.

<Theme id="factors" count="What moves it" title="What moves the price" />

### Overall size

A unit around the television and a cabinet below is a smaller project than cabinetry from wall to wall and floor to ceiling. Width and height decide how much material and fitting time it takes.

### Cabinetry and storage

Doors, drawers and enclosed cupboards add more work than open space. Storage for equipment, games and everyday clutter is usually where most of the cabinetry goes.

### Open shelving

Shelves either side of the television need to suit what goes on them. Books need more depth and stronger support than a few decorative pieces.

### Integrated lighting

Light strips along the shelves or around the television panel are planned with the cabinetry. That keeps the cables hidden and the power supply reachable for maintenance.

### Cable management

Cables from the television, the set-top box, speakers and consoles need routes through the unit. Planning them at the start keeps them out of sight and easy to change later.

### Ventilation

Equipment in a closed cupboard needs air around it. Openings, gaps or vented doors are designed in, and equipment sizes are checked before the openings are finalised.

### Specialist finishes and detailing

Painted finishes, real wood veneers, mouldings and panelling each add work. Melamine and decorative boards keep the cost down.

<Theme id="kinds" count="Two kinds" title="Two kinds of media wall" />

The words "media wall" cover two quite different things. One is a stud frame clad in plasterboard, usually with the television and sometimes an electric fire set into it, then plastered and painted.

The other is joinery: cabinetry, shelving and panelling fitted around the television, with doors, drawers and lighting. That is the kind we design and fit, and the kind this guide prices.

The two can be combined, with a plastered panel for the television framed by fitted cabinets. The scope then sets out which trade does which part.

<Theme id="scope" count="What is included" title="What is included, and what is agreed separately" />

The guide price covers the design, the cabinetry, delivery and fitting. Painting, lighting and electrical work should be clearly defined in the agreed scope, so there is no doubt later about who does what.

New sockets, wiring behind the unit or a supply for the lighting are work for an electrician. Mounting the television itself is agreed in the same way.

Doors, drawers and integrated components may need occasional adjustment over time. The guarantee covers installation and workmanship for 2 years and the structure of the joinery for 10 years. In the first 2 years we come back to adjust doors and drawers.

<Theme id="planning" count="Planning" title="Planning a media wall" />

A useful first brief is a photo of the whole wall and the size of your television. Add the equipment it needs to hold and a picture of a style you like. Measurements of the wall help, but the survey takes the exact ones.

Walls, floors and ceilings are rarely straight, so the cabinetry is scribed: cut to follow the line of the room rather than left with gaps. Around a chimney breast, the units can fill the alcoves on either side.
