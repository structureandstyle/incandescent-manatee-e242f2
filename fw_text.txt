A fitted wardrobe is measured against your walls, floor and ceiling, then designed around what you keep. The run uses the top of the room and both corners rather than losing them.
The inside is planned first: hanging heights, shelving, drawers and anything that needs a home. You do not need to arrive knowing any of that. Asking the right questions at the first visit is our job, not yours.
What do fitted wardrobes cost?
The honest answer is a range until the room is measured, and one visit turns it into a firm number.
Guide price
From £2,300 for a 2-door run up to 1.2m in melamine. Width and finish move it: from £4,000 at 4 doors, and painted, sprayed and oak veneer finishes step up from there. Internal drawers and integrated lighting add to any of them.
What moves it
Size and complexity: difficult room shapes, sloping ceilings, bespoke internal layouts, premium materials and veneers, specialist finishes, integrated lighting and extra detailing.
Smallest job taken
Generally £2,000 of project value.
How long does it take?
The lead time runs in weeks, and the fitting itself takes days rather than weeks.
Booked ahead
Lead time typically 4 to 8 weeks
Survey to drawings
Where the project needs design work, one week is set aside for it, and drawings follow at the end of that week. Straightforward projects can go straight to quotation.
Days on site
Typically 2 to 3 days for a straightforward wardrobe wall, closer to a week where on-site construction or hand-painting is involved.
What is it made from?
Which board suits a wardrobe wall depends on the room, the finish you want and the budget.
Materials
Standard and moisture-resistant MDF · veneered boards · plywood · melamine and decorative boards · high-gloss and super-matt boards · textured and wood-effect finishes · specialist decorative surfaces
Finishes
Spray-painted and hand-painted finishes, natural and stained veneers, melamine and decorative boards, and lacquered or oiled finishes where they suit the piece. The right one depends on the design, how the furniture is used, the budget and what it needs to match.
Hardware
Blum for hinges and drawer systems, Hafele for furniture hardware and lighting, and other specialist hardware where the project calls for it.
Why not solid timber
Solid timber rarely makes sense for fitted furniture. Engineered boards are more stable and more consistent, and a real wood veneer gives a natural timber finish where one is wanted. It also avoids using large amounts of solid hardwood to reach the same look and quality.
How is it fitted?
Ceilings, floors and walls are rarely straight, and a wardrobe wall meets all three at once.
Scribing
Walls, floors and ceilings are rarely straight, so the furniture is scribed, cut to follow the line of the room, rather than left with gaps. Fitted and finished, it should look like one piece that belongs in the room.
Clearing up
Floors and the surrounding area are protected before work starts, and dust extraction runs throughout. The area is tidied at the end of each day, and waste and off-cuts leave with us at the end.
What happens afterwards?
Doors and hinges do the daily work, so the cover on workmanship and moving parts is listed plainly.
Guarantee
2 years on installation and workmanship, 10 years on the structure and joinery, and the manufacturer&#39;s warranty on hinges, runners and other moving hardware.
Adjustments
Yes, doors and other moving parts that settle within the 2-year aftercare period are adjusted, and we come back to do it.
What a fitted wardrobe run includes
The work
The work covers wardrobe walls, alcove-to-alcove runs, and wardrobes that follow sloping ceilings and awkward room shapes. Doors are hinged on Blum hardware, in painted, veneered or decorative board finishes.
Inside, drawer banks run on Blum systems, and integrated lighting is wired in where the design calls for it. Veneer grain is matched across adjoining doors, so a run reads as one piece rather than a row of panels.
Cut to the wall as it actually is
Fit
Walls, floors and ceilings are rarely straight, so each piece is scribed: cut to follow the line of the room rather than left with gaps.
Fitting a straightforward wardrobe wall typically takes 2 to 3 days on site. Hand-painting or on-site construction can take it closer to a week, and you get the dates when the project is programmed.
Where we work
Service area
Across London, including Hampstead, Belsize Park, Primrose Hill, Kentish Town, Camden, Swiss Cottage, Highgate, West Hampstead, Islington and Maida Vale.
Asked before enquiring
Questions
Where is the furniture made?
+
Components are made to our drawings by specialist suppliers, then cut and fitted in your home by us. Which supplier depends on the job.
What guarantee comes with a fitted wardrobe?
+
The guarantee covers installation and workmanship for 2 years and the structure and joinery for 10 years. Hinges, runners and other moving hardware carry their manufacturer&#39;s warranty.
Will you come back if a door moves?
+
Yes, doors and other moving parts that settle within the 2-year aftercare period are adjusted, and we come back to do it.
How do I look after the finish?
+
That depends on the material and finish, so the care advice comes with the finished piece: how to clean it and what to avoid. Natural veneers develop subtly with age and light. Painted and decorative finishes stay more consistent.
